Modelling graduates: **885** (667 events), split once into **train 620** (467 events, EPV 29.19), **validation 133** (100 events) and **test 132** (100 events).

## Evaluation design (no data reuse)

- **One role per graduate, assigned once** (stratified by event and institution type; `core/splits.py`). No cross-validation: no graduate is ever used in more than one role.
- **Train:** the only data any model is fitted on, including the final model used by the app and its bootstrap refits. Preprocessing (imputation, scaling, encoding) is fitted on train only.
- **Validation:** hyperparameters are tuned and the candidate models compared here (fit on train, score on validation). AFT distributions are chosen by AIC on train.
- **Test (locked):** used once, after the model was chosen, to evaluate the selected model only. Nothing about the model was changed after seeing the test result.
- **Uno's C (primary)** and **integrated Brier score** are truncated at tau = the 90th percentile of training follow-up. Harrell's C is secondary. Intervals come from 500 bootstrap resamples of the evaluation set (no model is refitted).
- **Calibration** at 3, 6, 12 months: tertiles of predicted P(employed by t), mean predicted vs Kaplan-Meier observed; error = mean absolute difference.
- Classification metrics (accuracy, precision, recall, ROC-AUC) are deliberately not used.

## Model selection

**Rule:** choose the candidate (Cox PH, Elastic Net Cox, AFT best-by-AIC, DeepSurv) with the highest validation Uno's C. If another candidate is within 0.02 of it, prefer the simpler model (Cox PH = AFT < Elastic Net Cox < DeepSurv), then the better calibrated.

**Selected: AFT Log-Normal** - highest mean Uno's C (0.693); it was also the simplest / best-calibrated of the models within the margin.

## In plain terms (test set)

- Ranking: out of every 100 pairs of graduates, the model correctly picks which one found work first about 62 times (50 would be pure guessing, 100 would be perfect).
- Accuracy of the predicted shares: the share of graduates it predicted would be working within 3 months was, on average, within 15 percentage points of what actually happened.
- Accuracy of the predicted shares: the share of graduates it predicted would be working within 6 months was, on average, within 17 percentage points of what actually happened.
- Accuracy of the predicted shares: the share of graduates it predicted would be working within 12 months was, on average, within 8 percentage points of what actually happened.
- There is no single 'accuracy %': many graduates were still looking when surveyed, so their final outcome is unknown. The two measures above are the standard ones for this kind of data.
